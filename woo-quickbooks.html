WooCommerce and QuickBooks, so your shop's sales book themselves.

We connect your WooCommerce store to QuickBooks so orders, refunds and gateway payouts post on their own, with tax, shipping, coupons and fees in the right accounts. Because we build WooCommerce stores ourselves, we can fix problems at the source instead of patching around them.

The problem

Exporting orders and matching payouts is a job nobody should still be doing

Every week someone downloads WooCommerce orders, reshapes them in a spreadsheet and enters them into QuickBooks, then tries to work out which orders made up each Stripe or PayPal payout. It is slow, and the numbers rarely agree on the first pass.

Link the two and each order posts as an invoice or sales receipt, refunds become credit memos, and payouts are reconciled net of fees so the bank feed clears itself. Two things shape the build: your QuickBooks edition, and how many orders you take, which decides between per-order posting and a daily summary.

Done by hand

  • Orders exported and retyped
  • Refunds adjusted weeks later
  • Payouts matched order by order
  • Fees buried in net deposits

Posted automatically

  • Orders arrive as invoices or receipts
  • Refunds create credit memos
  • Payouts reconcile to their orders
  • Fees expensed separately

In practice

What lands in your books, and where

The mapping sheet you sign off lists every account. These are the areas your bookkeeper will care about most.

Sales and tax

  • Income
  • Sales tax
  • Shipping

Each order line goes to the right income account, tax to a liability account and shipping to its own line, so reports are clean.

Coupons and discounts

  • Coupon codes
  • Fees
  • Store credit

Discounts post on their own lines, so gross sales stay accurate and you can see what promotions cost.

Refunds

  • Full
  • Partial
  • Credit memo

Refunds are posted against the original sale as credit memos, including partial ones, so revenue and tax adjust correctly.

Payout matching

  • Stripe
  • PayPal
  • WooPayments

Payouts are posted net of fees, with the fee split to an expense account, so your bank feed clears without manual matching.

Customers and items

  • Matching
  • SKUs
  • Reports

Buyers match to existing customers and SKUs to QuickBooks items, which gives you product-level revenue reports.

Recurring revenue

  • Renewals
  • Schedules
  • Invoices

Subscription renewals post as scheduled invoices, so recurring income is visible in the same place as one-off sales.

Tax, shipping and discount plugins can change what an order contains, so we audit your live order data before we map anything.

Choosing a route

Four ways to build the link, and when each fits

The right route depends on your QuickBooks edition, your order volume and how customised your store is.

WooCommerce REST API and webhooksAuthenticated access plus order and refund events

Best when sales should post as they happen and you need full order detail.

Trade-off webhook reliability depends on hosting and caching.

WordPress plugin hookServer-side logic on order events

Best when a complex store needs its data shaped before it leaves.

Trade-off it lives in your codebase and must survive updates.

QuickBooks Online APICloud API secured with OAuth

Best when you run QuickBooks Online and want near real-time posting.

Trade-off rate limits, and a production app review before go-live.

Web ConnectorQuickBooks Desktop, via QBXML

Best when your company file runs on a local machine.

Trade-off it polls on a schedule and runs on the host computer.

Because we also build and maintain WooCommerce stores, we can fix problems in the store itself, such as hooks, data changes and plugin conflicts, instead of working around them in middleware.

How we work

From first call to monitored go-live

Every project follows the same five stages, and you approve the scope and the account mapping before anything is built.

  1. Discover

    We audit your store, extensions, tax setup and QuickBooks chart of accounts.

  2. Map

    Every field, account, direction and error rule is written down for sign-off.

  3. Build

    We develop on a staging site and a QuickBooks sandbox or test file, never live data.

  4. Test
