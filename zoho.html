The basics

What Zoho integration actually means

It means Zoho apps and outside software swap records on their own: leads, contacts, invoices and stock levels. A deal won in Zoho CRM becomes a QuickBooks invoice, and a Shopify order becomes a Zoho Inventory sales order, in seconds instead of hours.

Zoho is a suite, not one product, and most companies run several of its apps next to non-Zoho tools like accounting, a store and a phone system. So integration works in two directions: joining Zoho apps to each other cleanly, and reaching outward to everything else. The result is one picture of the business, less data entry and no copy-paste mistakes.

Choosing a route

Five ways to connect Zoho, and when each one fits

Zoho gives you REST APIs on every product, the Deluge scripting language, the Flow low-code tool and webhooks, and third-party platforms add ready-made connectors on top. The right choice depends on which apps are involved, your volumes and how standard your process is.

Zoho REST APIsOAuth 2.0, per product

Best when you need custom builds, high volumes or real-time sync.

Trade-off rate limits, API credits and per-product auth scopes.

Deluge functionsZoho's native scripting

Best when logic should run inside the suite, with light external calls.

Trade-off it runs inside Zoho only and isn't a full middleware tier.

Zoho FlowLow-code app-to-app

Best when a standard flow should go live fast with no code.

Trade-off templates bend only so far, so unusual logic needs Deluge.

iPaaS connectorsMake, Zapier or n8n

Best when the flow is common, like CRM to accounting or store orders.

Trade-off a subscription forever and limited transformation depth.

Custom middlewareMake, n8n or code

Best when several systems or unusual rules are involved.

Trade-off you need a partner to maintain it.

For many standard flows, Zoho Flow or a ready-made connector is the right call. We'll say so and help you set it up, and only write custom Deluge or middleware when your process demands it.
