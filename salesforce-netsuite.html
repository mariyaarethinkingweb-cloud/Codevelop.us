The problem

Why the pipeline and the ledger keep disagreeing

Salesforce knows who is buying. NetSuite knows what has shipped, what has been billed and what has been paid. With nothing between them, someone copies each won deal into a NetSuite order by hand, and every "where is my order?" from a rep becomes a message to finance.

The integration keeps customer records aligned both ways, creates NetSuite sales orders from approved quotes and closed-won opportunities, and writes fulfilment, invoice and payment status back onto the Salesforce account. One early design choice matters most: your Salesforce API allocation. Per-record REST calls are simple but use up daily limits fast, while high-volume work belongs in scheduled Bulk API jobs, so we size that against your edition before we pick an approach.

Systems apart

  • Won deals retyped into NetSuite
  • Reps emailing finance for order status
  • Duplicate customers in both systems
  • Credit problems found after a promise is made

Systems linked

  • Orders created from closed-won deals
  • Status visible on the Salesforce account
  • One matched customer record
  • Credit holds flagged before quoting dates

After go-live

One closed-won deal, from CRM to cash

Once the link is running, a typical deal moves through both systems like this, with nobody retyping anything.

  1. Deal won

    A rep marks the opportunity closed-won, or a quote is approved, and a matching check runs against existing NetSuite customers.

  2. Order created

    A NetSuite sales order is built with the right lines, prices, terms and subsidiary, and the order number is written back to Salesforce.

  3. Goods shipped

    When fulfilment is posted in NetSuite, the ship date and tracking number appear on the Salesforce account.

  4. Invoice issued

    The invoice amount and due date are shown to sales and customer success, so they can answer billing questions themselves.

  5. Payment received

    The balance and payment status update on the account, closing the loop from pipeline to cash.

Choosing a route

How we build the Salesforce link

Both platforms have capable APIs, so the real decisions are orchestration and call budget. That is where many projects are sized wrongly.

RouteWhat it isSuitsKeep in mind
Salesforce REST APIA JSON API for real-time, record-level operations.Opportunity-to-order flows at low or moderate volumeDaily call limits depend on edition, and heavy use exhausts them
Salesforce Bulk API 2.0Asynchronous loading and extraction of large record sets.Nightly account syncs, backfills and big cataloguesNot real-time, because jobs queue before they run
NetSuite SuiteTalk RESTModern REST access to NetSuite records.Creating orders and reading invoice statusGovernance limits apply to high-volume bursts
RESTlets / SuiteScriptCustom NetSuite endpoints written in SuiteScript.Non-standard records and heavy transformationThe code lives inside NetSuite and needs upkeep
iPaaS connectorsPrebuilt templates on platforms such as Celigo, Boomi or Workato.Standard opportunity-to-order flows that must launch fastThe subscription never ends, and templates only bend so far

An honest note: if your flow really is opportunity to sales order to invoice status, a prebuilt connector can be the right answer, and we will help you configure it. Custom middleware earns its cost when approval rules, several subsidiaries or non-standard objects come into play.

Our process

Five stages, with a signed mapping sheet before we build

You receive a fixed-price scope and approve the mapping sheet before any configuration starts.

  1. Discover

    We review your Salesforce objects, NetSuite records, API allocation and which system owns what.

  2. Map

    Every field, direction, matching rule and error case is written down before building.

  3. Build

    We build against a NetSuite sandbox and a Salesforce sandbox.

  4. Test

    Real deals run end to end, including amendments, cancellations, duplicates and limit handling.

    After launch we monitor with logging and alerts, and you get a named contact.
