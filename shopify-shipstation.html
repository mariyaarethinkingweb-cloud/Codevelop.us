The basics

What Shopify and ShipStation integration does

It is the automatic flow of orders out to fulfilment and tracking data back into Shopify. ShipStation already has a built-in Shopify connection, so for most merchants the job isn't building a connector. It is configuring routing rules, carrier logic and multi-warehouse splits so the right order reaches the right warehouse with the right service every time.

Shipping a parcel is rarely the hard part. Routing is. Multiple warehouses, split shipments, backorders, and marketplace orders arriving alongside direct sales all need rules that a default ShipStation setup doesn't cover. A properly scoped configuration, or a middleware layer, usually pays for itself quickly in less manual sorting.

Routing rules

What "written-down" routing looks like

In many warehouses, routing lives in someone's head. We turn it into explicit if-then rules. These examples are illustrative, and yours come from your own fulfilment map.

By destination

  • Country
  • State
  • Zone

West-coast addresses go to the nearest node, and international orders go to the warehouse that holds the customs paperwork.

By product

  • SKU
  • Tag
  • Weight

Heavy or oversized items route to the freight-capable site, and fragile lines get their own packing queue.

By order type

  • Marketplace
  • Wholesale
  • Backorder

Marketplace orders follow their own carrier rules, and backorders are held or split instead of marking the whole order shipped.

Undocumented exceptions are the biggest schedule risk, so we dig them out during discovery, before any rule is configured.

After go-live

One order, from checkout to doorstep

Once the rules are live, a typical order moves without anyone sorting or copying.

  1. Order placed

    A customer checks out, and the order lands in ShipStation with items, address and chosen shipping method.

  2. Rules applied

    Routing assigns it to the right warehouse, tags it and splits it if the items live in different places.

  3. Label created

    A carrier label is generated at your negotiated rate, based on weight and destination.

  4. Tracking returned

    The tracking number flows back to Shopify, which sends the buyer their shipping confirmation.

  5. Stock updated

    Available quantity is synced so the storefront never promises what has already gone.
