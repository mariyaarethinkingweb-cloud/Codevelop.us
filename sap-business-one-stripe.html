The problem

Why Stripe money is hard to see in B1

Stripe knows the moment a customer pays. B1 only knows what somebody tells it. Until the two are linked, an accountant exports the payout report, finds the matching invoice for each charge, and works out the processing fee on every line before anything is posted.

Our integration listens to Stripe events such as paid charges, refunds, disputes and payouts, then writes them into B1 as incoming payments, credit memos and journal entries. Because Stripe sends many payments to the bank as a single deposit, we also break that deposit back into its parts, so the bank line ties out to the cent.

Systems apart

  • Payout reports exported and sorted by hand
  • Fees worked out line by line
  • Refunds and chargebacks found late
  • Bank deposits that never quite match

Systems linked

  • Payments post to B1 as they happen
  • Fees split to the right expense account
  • Refunds and disputes tracked on the invoice
  • Each deposit reconciled to its charges
Entity mapping

What syncs between Stripe and SAP B1, and which way

Most projects move the same core events. We adjust the account mapping and currency handling to suit your chart of accounts and the way you bill.

On StripeIn SAP B1DirectionWhat travels
Successful chargesIncoming payments (ORCT) applied to A/R invoices (OINV)Stripe → B1Amount, currency, customer reference, payment method
RefundsA/R credit memos (ORIN)Stripe → B1Refund amount, reason, link to the original invoice
DisputesFlags and journal entries (OJDT)Stripe → B1Chargeback status, held amount, evidence deadline
Processing feesExpense account postings (OJDT)Stripe → B1Fee per transaction, gross-to-net difference
PayoutsBank statement lines and reconciliation (OBNK)Stripe → B1Deposit total, date, and the charges and fees inside it
Payment links and hosted invoicesOpen A/R invoices (OINV)B1 → StripeInvoice amount, due date, customer, payment link
SubscriptionsScheduled A/R invoices and customers (OCRD)Both waysBilling cycles, plan changes, customer records

These are the usual directions. We change them to fit how you charge, for example by routing different Stripe accounts or currencies to different B1 bank accounts.

After go-live

One Stripe payment, from card tap to bank statement

With the link running, a typical payment moves through the books like this, and nobody opens a payout report.

  1. Customer pays

    Stripe confirms the charge and sends an event to our listener, which finds the matching B1 invoice.

  2. Payment posted

    An incoming payment is created against the invoice, and the processing fee is booked to its own expense account.

  3. Refund or dispute

    A refund becomes a credit memo, and a chargeback is flagged on the original invoice before it can be forgotten.

  4. Payout created

    Stripe bundles settled payments into one transfer, and we fetch the list of transactions that make it up.

  5. Deposit reconciled

    The bank line is matched to those transactions and fees, so the statement balances without manual work.

Choosing a route

How we build the Stripe link

Stripe offers webhooks for live events and a REST API for lookups. We pair them with the SAP B1 Service Layer or DI API, depending on your B1 version and hosting.

RouteWhat it isSuitsKeep in mind
Stripe webhooksPush notifications for charges, refunds, disputes and payouts.Posting payments the moment they happenNeeds a dependable listener with retries and duplicate protection
Stripe REST APIOn-demand access to charges, balance transactions and payouts.Backfills, reconciliation runs and dispute detailRate limits apply on busy accounts
SAP B1 Service Layer / DI APIInterfaces for creating B1 business documents.Writing payments, credit memos and journal entriesPayment means and GL accounts must be configured first
iPaaS toolsPrebuilt flows in tools such as Zapier, Make or n8n.Simple one-way payment logging that must launch quicklyFee splitting and payout matching usually still need custom code

Where templates usually fall short: a generic tool can record a single charge in B1, but matching one bank deposit to dozens of underlying payments, with every fee separated correctly, is where templated builds run out of road and custom logic begins.
