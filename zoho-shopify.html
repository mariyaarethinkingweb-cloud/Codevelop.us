We connect your Shopify store to Zoho Inventory, Books and CRM so orders arrive as sales documents, stock stays honest across warehouses, and shipment tracking returns to the customer. The storefront stays fast, and the back office stops being a second job.

The problem

Why a good storefront still creates a manual back office

Shopify runs the shop window. Behind it, Zoho usually handles stock, accounting or customer records. With no link, every order becomes a sales order someone creates by hand, and every stock change has to be copied back into Shopify before the next customer buys the last unit.

The integration turns each Shopify order into a Zoho sales order or invoice, keeps stock counts agreed in both directions, and carries customers over so support and sales see the same purchase history that Shopify shows. Shipping status travels the other way, so shoppers get accurate tracking without anyone sending an email.

Systems apart

  • Orders retyped as Zoho sales orders
  • Stock edited by hand in two places
  • Overselling the last few units
  • Tracking sent to customers manually

Systems linked

  • Orders arrive in Zoho automatically
  • One stock figure per warehouse
  • Low stock reflected before it sells out
  • Tracking numbers flow back to Shopify

After go-live

One Shopify order, from checkout to a tracked shipment

With the link running, a typical order moves through your systems like this, and nobody keys it in twice.

  1. Order placed

    A shopper checks out, and a Shopify webhook sends the order to the integration within moments.

  2. Sales order created

    Zoho receives a sales order or invoice with the customer, lines and tax, and the customer is matched to avoid duplicates.

  3. Stock allocated

    The right warehouse is reduced, and the new available quantity is pushed to Shopify so the next buyer sees the real figure.

  4. Shipment packed

    When the package is marked shipped in Zoho, the carrier and tracking number are captured.

  5. Customer notified

    Tracking flows back to Shopify, which sends the shipping email, and any later refund returns stock and credit to Zoho.

Choosing a route

How we build the Shopify link

Shopify offers a GraphQL and REST Admin API with webhooks, and Zoho offers REST APIs plus Deluge. The mix depends on order volume and how much logic sits between them.

RouteWhat it isSuitsKeep in mind
Shopify Admin API and webhooksGraphQL and REST access plus real-time order and stock events.Immediate order and inventory updates from the storeWebhook delivery needs retry handling on our side
Zoho REST API and DelugeZoho's own APIs and native scripting.Creating sales orders, invoices and stock adjustmentsDeluge alone handles large batch syncs poorly
Zoho FlowZoho's low-code automation tool with a Shopify connector.Standard order flows that must launch fastNon-standard logic still needs Deluge or middleware
Custom middlewareOrchestration built over both APIs.High-volume stores and multi-warehouse inventory rulesNeeds a partner who can maintain it

An honest note: for a single store with standard order flows, Zoho Flow's Shopify connector is often enough. We will say so, and only build custom middleware when your order volume or inventory rules truly need it.
