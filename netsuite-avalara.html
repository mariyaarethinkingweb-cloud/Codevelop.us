NetSuite and Avalara, so every order carries the right tax.

We connect Avalara AvaTax to NetSuite so sales tax is worked out line by line the moment a document is saved, exemption certificates are checked for you, and your returns draw on system data instead of a spreadsheet.

The basics

What this integration actually does

Sales tax gets complicated the moment you sell across a state line. Rates differ by jurisdiction and product type, nexus thresholds shift, and a single wrong rate on a busy SKU can turn into an assessment months later.

Here, NetSuite calls Avalara AvaTax each time a sales order, invoice or credit memo is saved, so every line carries the correct tax. Customer addresses are validated, exemption certificates are checked before any zero-rate sale, and posted transactions are committed to Avalara so returns are built from real data.

Without integration

  • Rates typed or looked up by hand
  • Exemption certificates tracked in a spreadsheet
  • Credit memos that don't reverse tax cleanly
  • Returns assembled from exports

With integration

  • Line-level tax on every document
  • Certificates applied automatically
  • Credits reverse the original tax
  • Returns prepared from committed data
Save to filing

One document, five steps, no manual tax work

  1. Document saved

    A user or a connected store saves an order, invoice or credit memo in NetSuite.

  2. Address checked

    The customer address is validated to the correct taxing jurisdiction.

  3. Exemption tested

    Valid certificates are applied, and missing or expiring ones are flagged.

  4. Tax calculated

    Avalara returns line-level rates, and NetSuite stores them on the document.

  5. Transaction committed

    The posted document is committed so returns come from real data.

Data flow

What syncs, and which way

Most NetSuite and Avalara projects cover the same six areas. Product taxability mapping is the part that varies most from one business to the next.

Tax calculation

  • NetSuite → Avalara → NetSuite

Line-level rates come back as orders, invoices and credit memos are saved.

Address validation

  • NetSuite → Avalara

Customer addresses are standardised to the right taxing jurisdiction.

Exemption certificates

  • Avalara → NetSuite

Valid certificates apply automatically, and expiring ones are flagged.

Product taxability

  • NetSuite → Avalara

Item records map to Avalara tax codes so each category rates correctly.

Committed transactions

  • NetSuite → Avalara

Posted documents are committed for reporting and return preparation.

Credits & adjustments

  • NetSuite → Avalara

Refunds and credit memos reverse the original tax cleanly.

Choosing a route

Four ways to build it, and when each one fits

Avalara offers a NetSuite SuiteApp that handles standard cases well. Custom work is about the edges: unusual document types, custom records and taxability rules the SuiteApp doesn't model.

AvaTax SuiteAppAvalara's own bundle in NetSuite

Best when you sell on standard transaction types with ordinary sales tax.

Trade-off custom records and unusual flows fall outside its scope.

AvaTax REST APIDirect calculation and commit calls

Best when you have custom document types, outside order sources or bespoke logic.

Trade-off you own the retry, timeout and fallback behaviour.

SuiteScript user eventsLogic that runs as records save

Best when taxability rules and overrides must apply the same way every time.

Trade-off script speed matters on high-volume order entry.

CertCaptureAvalara exemption certificates

Best when you have many exempt or reseller customers to manage.

Trade-off the quality of your certificate data decides how well it works.

For a straightforward US sales tax setup, the SuiteApp plus careful tax code mapping is usually enough, and we'll say so. Custom work earns its cost when you have custom transaction types, unusual taxability or order sources outside NetSuite.
