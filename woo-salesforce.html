The basics

What this integration actually does

A store and a CRM each see half of the same customer. WooCommerce knows what they bought, while Salesforce knows the conversations, cases and campaigns. Without a link, agents open WP Admin in another tab, and marketing segments on data that's weeks old.

This integration creates or matches every WooCommerce buyer as a Salesforce contact or account, writes their order history against that record, keeps refund and fulfilment status current, and shows lifetime value so sales and service share one view. The key design question is Salesforce's API allocation: a busy store burns through per-record calls quickly, so above a certain volume we batch through Bulk API 2.0 instead, sized against your edition.

Choosing a route

Four ways to build it, and when each one fits

WooCommerce pushes events through webhooks or PHP hooks, and Salesforce takes them through REST or Bulk depending on volume. Busy stores need that decision made early.

WooCommerce REST API and webhooksAuthenticated access plus live events

Best when orders and customers should sync the moment they happen.

Trade-off delivery depends on your hosting, caching and cron setup.

WordPress plugin hooksPHP inside the store

Best when data needs shaping or enriching before it reaches Salesforce.

Trade-off the code lives in your site and must survive core updates.

Salesforce REST APIReal-time record-level calls

Best when you sync order by order at low to moderate volume.

Trade-off daily call limits depend on your edition, and heavy volume burns through them.

Salesforce Bulk API 2.0Asynchronous batches

Best when you need historical backfills or high-volume daily syncs.

Trade-off jobs queue and process asynchronously, so it isn't real-time.

The decision that shapes everything is per-order sync versus daily batch. A store doing thousands of orders a day will exhaust Salesforce limits on per-record calls, so we size it against your edition before choosing.
