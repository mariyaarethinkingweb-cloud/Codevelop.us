The basics

What NetSuite integration actually means

It means NetSuite and the rest of your software pass data to each other automatically: customers, items, sales orders, invoices and stock counts. A Shopify checkout becomes a NetSuite sales order, and a 3PL shipment becomes an item fulfilment, within minutes instead of days.

Most NetSuite companies also run a CRM, a storefront, an EDI partner, a tax engine and a warehouse platform, and each one keeps its own copy of the truth. Connect them and your team works from one picture, with far less typing and none of the mistakes that creep in when records are copied by hand.

Without integration

  • Web orders keyed into NetSuite by hand
  • Stock counts that are always a day behind
  • Tracking numbers pasted in manually
  • Tax and invoices that don't match

With integration

  • Orders arrive in NetSuite on their own
  • Live inventory across every channel
  • Shipments update the order automatically
  • One record of every sale, everywhere
Order to fulfilment

One order, five handoffs, nothing retyped

  1. Order placed

    A customer buys on your store, marketplace or EDI feed.

  2. Sales order created

    NetSuite receives the customer, items, prices and terms.

  3. Tax applied

    The tax engine calculates the right amount before invoicing.

  4. Warehouse ships

    The 3PL picks the order and sends back tracking and stock.

  5. Status flows back

    The store, CRM and customer see the update without a call.

Choosing a route

Five ways to connect NetSuite, and when each one fits

NetSuite integration runs on the SuiteCloud platform: SuiteTalk web services, custom RESTlets written in SuiteScript, and iPaaS tools like Celigo and Boomi that wrap those APIs in templates. Your record types, order volume, governance limits and process decide which is right.

SuiteTalk RESTModern JSON web services

Best when you're starting a new build or need real-time flows.

Trade-off a few older record types aren't fully covered yet.

SuiteTalk SOAPMature XML and WSDL

Best when you need wide record coverage, bulk work or legacy systems.

Trade-off heavier payloads and older developer tooling.

RESTlets and SuiteScriptCustom logic inside NetSuite

Best when your data shape or rules don't fit the standard APIs.

Trade-off it needs real SuiteScript skill and respects governance limits.

iPaaS connectorsCeligo or Boomi templates

Best when your flow is standard and you want it live fast.

Trade-off the subscription never ends, and templates only bend so far.

Custom middlewareMake, n8n or code

Best when several systems, unusual rules or AI automation are involved.

Trade-off you need a partner to look after it.

For many standard NetSuite flows, a ready-made connector like Celigo is the right answer. We'll say so, help you configure it, and write custom SuiteScript only when your process demands it.
