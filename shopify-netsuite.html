The basics

What Shopify and NetSuite integration actually means

It means orders, stock, items, customers and shipments move between your Shopify store and NetSuite on their own. Instead of exporting CSVs or keying sales orders by hand, the integration creates the order the moment checkout finishes, with stock, tax and shipping details attached.

Once a merchant outgrows spreadsheets or a smaller accounting package, NetSuite usually becomes the system of record for the ledger, the item master and often the warehouses. Shopify stays the home of the buying experience. The integration keeps both honest, so you don't oversell, invoices match what the customer paid, and finance isn't reconciling two versions of one order at month-end.

Without integration

  • Sales orders keyed in by hand
  • Overselling when stock lags
  • Tracking pasted back manually
  • Two versions of every order at month-end

With integration

  • Checkout creates the NetSuite order
  • Stock updated near real time
  • Tracking flows to the customer
  • Invoices and payouts that match
Checkout to books

One order, five handoffs, nothing re-keyed

  1. Order placed

    A shopper checks out on your Shopify store.

  2. Sales order created

    NetSuite receives the buyer, items, tax, shipping and discounts.

  3. Stock adjusted

    Available quantity updates per SKU and location, so you don't oversell.

  4. Shipment confirmed

    The fulfilment and tracking number are pushed back to Shopify.

  5. Payment reconciled

    Payouts and refunds are matched against NetSuite invoices.

Data flow

What syncs between Shopify and NetSuite

Most projects sync the same six things. Direction and frequency depend on which system owns each piece of data.

Orders

  • Shopify → NetSuite

New orders become sales orders with line items, tax, shipping and discounts.

Inventory levels

  • NetSuite → Shopify

Available-to-sell stock per SKU and location, updated near real time.

Items and products

  • NetSuite ↔ Shopify

SKU, price and attributes stay aligned, and new items can start on either side.

Customers

  • Shopify → NetSuite

Buyer records are created or matched, so you don't get duplicates.

Fulfilment and tracking

  • NetSuite → Shopify

Ship confirmations and tracking numbers go back so Shopify can email the customer.

Payments and invoices

  • Shopify → NetSuite

Payouts and refunds reconcile against NetSuite invoices for accurate financials.

Choosing a route

Four ways to connect Shopify to NetSuite, and when each fits

There's no single official connector, only several credible paths. The right one depends on order volume, how many subsidiaries and locations you run in NetSuite, and how standard your fulfilment flow is.

SuiteCommerce ConnectorNetSuite's own storefront bundle

Best when your team is NetSuite-centric and wants first-party support.

Trade-off licensing cost, and less flexibility for Shopify-specific logic.

iPaaS connectorsCeligo or Alumio templates

Best when your order-to-cash flow is standard and you want it live fast.

Trade-off the subscription never ends, and customisation has limits.

SuiteScript and RESTletsCustom code on both sides

Best when you run multiple subsidiaries, currencies or unusual business rules.

Trade-off you need a partner who can maintain SuiteScript long term.

Custom middlewareMake, n8n or code

Best when NetSuite must connect to other systems, such as a 3PL or CRM, in one flow.

Trade-off more moving parts to monitor after go-live.

If your order volume is standard and NetSuite is a single subsidiary, an iPaaS template is often the fastest and cheapest path. We'll say so, even though it means less build work for us.
