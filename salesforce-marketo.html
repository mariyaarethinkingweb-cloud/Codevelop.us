The problem

Why "qualified" means something different to each team

Marketo and Salesforce already share a native sync, and for plain lead and contact mirroring it does the job. What it cannot do is end the argument: marketing says it passed on qualified leads, sales says they were not, and nobody has the revenue figures to prove either side.

Our work starts by correcting the sync you already have, including field mappings, lifecycle stages and routing rules. On top of that foundation we build the missing layer: engagement-based scoring that sales accepts, campaign membership that carries through lead conversion, and closed-loop attribution from first touch to closed-won revenue.

Systems apart

  • Scores sales does not believe
  • Campaign history lost at conversion
  • Lifecycle stages that differ per tool
  • Reports counting leads, not revenue

Systems linked

  • Scoring built on agreed rules
  • Campaign membership kept after conversion
  • One lifecycle model in both systems
  • Closed revenue attributed to campaigns

After go-live

One lead, from first click to closed-won revenue

With the link tuned and extended, a typical lead travels through both systems like this, and every step stays on the record.

  1. First touch

    A visitor fills in a form, becomes a Marketo person, and is tied to the program that brought them in.

  2. Engagement scored

    Opens, visits and form fills raise the score, and the score appears on the Salesforce lead for the rep to see.

  3. Handed to sales

    When the agreed threshold is met, the lead becomes an MQL and is routed to the right owner by the shared rules.

  4. Converted

    The lead becomes a contact and opportunity, and the campaign membership comes with it instead of disappearing.

  5. Revenue attributed

    When the deal closes, the revenue is credited back to the campaigns that sourced it, ready for reporting.
