The problem

Why a paid Stripe charge is still unpaid in the books

Stripe knows a customer has paid the moment it happens, but that means nothing in QuickBooks until someone applies it. Without a link, an accountant downloads the payout report, hunts for the invoice behind each charge, and separates the processing fee from the gross figure every single time.

The integration listens for Stripe events (successful charges, refunds, disputes and payouts) and applies them in QuickBooks. Payments are received against open invoices, refunds become credit memos, fees go to a dedicated expense account, and one payout entry ties the bundled bank deposit back to the transactions inside it.

Systems apart

  • Invoices left open after customers paid
  • Charges matched to invoices by hand
  • Fees mixed into the gross amount
  • Deposits that will not tie to the bank

Systems linked

  • Payments applied to the right invoice
  • Fees posted to their own account
  • Refunds and disputes tracked on the invoice
  • Each payout cleared against the bank feed

After go-live

One Stripe payment, from invoice link to cleared bank line

With the link running, a typical payment moves through your books like this, and nobody opens a payout report.

  1. Invoice sent

    An open QuickBooks invoice goes to the customer with a Stripe payment link, or the customer pays through your checkout.

  2. Payment received

    Stripe confirms the charge, and the event reaches the integration, which finds the matching invoice by reference.

  3. Invoice closed

    The payment is applied in QuickBooks and the invoice status changes to paid, with the fee booked to its own account.

  4. Refund or dispute

    A refund creates a credit memo, and a dispute is flagged against the original invoice before it can be overlooked.

  5. Payout cleared

    Stripe sends one deposit, and we match it to the charges and fees behind it so the bank line clears without manual work.

Where templates usually fall short: recording one Stripe charge in QuickBooks is easy. Matching a bundled payout back to dozens of transactions, with fees split correctly so the deposit clears, is where generic automation stops and real logic begins.
