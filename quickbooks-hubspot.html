The problem

Why HubSpot's revenue numbers rarely match the books

HubSpot is where the relationship and the pipeline live, and QuickBooks is where the money arrives. When the two stay apart, someone retypes each won deal as an invoice, and marketing reports on a pipeline that nobody can tie back to cash.

The integration keeps companies and contacts matched between the two tools, turns won deals and approved quotes into QuickBooks invoices, and writes invoice, payment and lifetime revenue figures onto the HubSpot company record. That closes the gap between "deal value" and "money in the bank", which is the number a leadership team actually wants.

Systems apart

  • Won deals retyped as invoices
  • Revenue estimated from deal amounts
  • Campaigns credited for unpaid deals
  • Sales unaware of overdue accounts

Systems linked

  • Invoices created from closed-won deals
  • Real invoiced revenue on the company
  • Reports based on paid amounts
  • Payment status visible on the timeline

After go-live

One HubSpot deal, from closed-won to revenue on the company record

With the link running, a typical deal moves through both systems like this, with nobody retyping anything.

  1. Deal closes

    A deal reaches closed-won, and the company is matched against existing QuickBooks customers before anything is created.

  2. Approval step

    If you want one, a finance reviewer approves the draft before it posts. Otherwise the invoice goes straight through.

  3. Invoice created

    QuickBooks receives the invoice with mapped lines and terms, and its number returns to the HubSpot deal.

  4. Payment tracked

    Paid, partly paid and overdue status appear on the company timeline as finance records each payment.

  5. Revenue reported

    Invoiced and collected totals update the company's revenue properties, ready for lifecycle stages and campaign reports.

Choosing a route

How we build the HubSpot link

HubSpot's CRM API and webhooks pair neatly with QuickBooks. As ever, the QuickBooks edition decides how that side is reached.

RouteWhat it isSuitsKeep in mind
HubSpot CRM API and webhooksREST access to CRM objects plus event subscriptions.Triggering on a deal stage change and syncing associationsRate limits vary by HubSpot tier
QuickBooks Online APIA cloud REST API secured with OAuth 2.0.Creating invoices and reading payment status in QBORate limits apply, and the app needs review before go-live
Web Connector (Desktop, QBXML)An Intuit tool that swaps QBXML with the local company file.QuickBooks Desktop reached from a cloud CRMRuns on a polling schedule from the host machine
iPaaS connectorsPrebuilt templates on tools such as Zapier, Make or Celigo.Standard deal-to-invoice flows that must launch fastAn ongoing subscription, and templates only bend so far

Worth knowing: HubSpot's own accounting sync handles simple contact and invoice mirroring perfectly well. A real integration only becomes necessary for deal-to-invoice conversion, quote logic and sending revenue back for attribution.
