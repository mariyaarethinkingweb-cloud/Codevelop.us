The problem

Why Amazon and B1 drift apart

Amazon holds the order and the buyer. B1 holds the stock, the cost and the financials. With no link between them, somebody downloads order reports and retypes them into the ERP, or edits Amazon stock counts by hand before the day starts. That gap is where oversells, missed refunds and late shipments come from.

The integration brings new Amazon orders into B1 as sales orders or A/R invoices, pushes B1 stock back so listings stop overselling, and matches Amazon settlement reports (fees, refunds, reimbursements) to incoming payments. Finance closes the month without a spreadsheet in sight.

Systems apart

  • Order reports retyped into B1
  • Stock edited by hand each morning
  • Fees and refunds matched manually
  • Overselling when counts lag

Systems linked

  • Orders land in B1 automatically
  • One live stock figure for every listing
  • Payouts reconciled to the cent
  • Refunds and returns booked correctly

Most projects share the same core records. We tune the field mapping and the frequency to your fulfilment model and the marketplaces you sell in.

After go-live

One Amazon order, from checkout to bank deposit

Once the link is running, a typical order looks like this, with nobody downloading a report or touching a spreadsheet.

  1. Order placed

    A buyer checks out on Amazon, and the order is pulled into B1 as a sales order or invoice within minutes.

  2. Stock adjusted

    The correct warehouse is reduced, and the new quantity, minus your buffer, is pushed to every marketplace listing.

  3. Shipment confirmed

    For merchant-fulfilled orders, the carrier and tracking number from B1 return to Amazon so the buyer is notified.

  4. Refund or return

    A return becomes a credit memo and restock in B1, so the stock count and the books stay right.

  5. Payout reconciled

    The settlement report is parsed and matched against B1 incoming payments, fee by fee.

Choosing a route

How we build the Amazon link

Amazon's Selling Partner API (SP-API) is the OAuth-based interface that replaced the old MWS API. We pair it with the same B1 interfaces we use on our other SAP projects.

RouteWhat it isSuitsKeep in mind
Amazon SP-APIA REST API for orders, inventory feeds, pricing and reports.Live order pull and scheduled stock pushRate limits per endpoint, and report data arrives with a delay
SAP B1 Service LayerA REST/OData API for B1 business objects.Creating orders, invoices and stock changes in B1Needs real development skill, and is HANA-first
Settlement report parsingScheduled ingestion of Amazon's financial reports.Matching fees and payouts to B1 paymentsFormats differ by marketplace and change from time to time
iPaaS connectorsPrebuilt templates such as Celigo, CedCommerce or Codisto.Standard order and stock sync that must launch fastAn endless subscription, and settlement logic often needs custom work anyway

Where templates usually fall short: most connectors do orders and inventory well but treat settlement reconciliation as an afterthought. If matching fees and payouts to the cent matters to your finance team, budget for a custom piece there, even when the rest runs on a connector.
