The basics

What a Shopify and SAP B1 link really means

It is an automatic exchange of items, stock, orders, customers and deliveries between your Shopify store and your B1 database. B1 exposes that data through the Service Layer (a REST API) and through B1i, its integration framework, and a good build uses one of them to turn a Shopify checkout into a B1 sales order the moment it happens.

B1 tends to be the ERP of mid-sized manufacturers, distributors and multi-warehouse retailers who need proper stock, batch or serial control that a lighter accounting tool can't give them. Shopify is the shop window on top. Without a link, a person on your team creates sales orders by hand and edits web stock manually, which stops working the moment order volume climbs.

Entity mapping

What travels between Shopify and SAP B1

Six B1 objects cover the typical project. Direction follows whichever system owns the data, and B1 is almost always the source of truth for stock and pricing.

In SAP B1In ShopifyDirectionWhat moves
Items (OITM)Products and variantsB1 → ShopifyItem master, pricing and attributes published to the catalog
Warehouse stock (OITW)Inventory levels by locationB1 → ShopifyAvailable quantity per warehouse, so you don't oversell
Sales orders (ORDR)OrdersShopify → B1Line items, tax and shipping from each checkout
Business partners (OCRD)CustomersShopify → B1Buyers created or matched as B1 customer records
Deliveries (ODLN)Fulfilments and trackingB1 → ShopifyDelivery documents and tracking numbers that trigger shipping emails
A/R invoices (OINV)Payouts and paymentsReconciledInvoices raised in B1 matched against Shopify payouts

If your price lists, tax codes or bin locations are unusual, the mapping bends to suit them. Nothing is forced into a template.

After go-live

One web order, from basket to delivered

With the link running, a normal order moves through your systems like this, and your team only steps in for the exceptions.

  1. Checkout completed

    A customer pays on Shopify, and a B1 sales order is created with its lines, tax and shipping.

  2. Customer matched

    The buyer is matched to an existing business partner or created as a new one, with no duplicates.

  3. Stock reserved

    The right warehouse is committed in B1, and the new available quantity is pushed to the storefront.

  4. Delivery posted

    Your warehouse ships, and the B1 delivery and tracking number go back to Shopify, which emails the buyer.

  5. Invoice reconciled

    The A/R invoice is matched to the Shopify payout so finance closes without a spreadsheet.

Choosing a route

Four ways to connect Shopify to SAP Business One

B1 has two native integration surfaces, the Service Layer REST API and B1i, plus iPaaS connectors built on top of them. The right pick depends on your B1 version, whether you host on premise or in the cloud, and how complex your warehouses are.

RouteWhat it isSuitsKeep in mind
Service LayerB1's modern REST interface to items, sales orders and partners.Custom builds that need full control of B1 objectsNeeds real B1 development knowledge to do well
B1iSAP's built-in integration framework and message broker.On-premise shops that want a supported, native pathHeavy on configuration, with a steeper learning curve
iPaaS connectorPrebuilt Shopify to B1 templates on platforms like Alumio or Celigo.Standard order and stock sync that must launch fastA recurring subscription, and templates only flex so far
Custom middlewareAn orchestration layer calling the Service Layer and Shopify APIs directly.Multi-warehouse, batch or serial tracking, bespoke pricingNeeds a partner who knows both B1 and Shopify

A straight answer: if you run one warehouse with standard pricing, an iPaaS template will go live faster and cost less than a custom Service Layer build. We will recommend it even though it means less work for us.
