QuickBooks and your POS, so the day's takings book themselves.

We connect your point of sale to QuickBooks so every trading day posts without anyone retyping the till report. Sales, tax, tips, discounts and card fees land in the right accounts, and the deposit that reaches your bank matches what the books say it should.

The problem

The till report is right, and the books are still wrong

Your POS knows what you sold, but it reports one lump. Someone then has to split it into revenue, sales tax, tips, discounts and fees, and type it into QuickBooks. Skip a day and the bank feed stops matching, and month end becomes detective work.

Connect them and each day posts as a clean summary. Revenue goes to the right categories, tax to a liability account, tips to their own account, and the net card deposit clears against what the bank shows. Multi-location businesses get one summary per site.

In practice

The tricky parts of a trading day, handled

The connection is the easy part. The day's close is where books go wrong, so we map it explicitly.

Revenue split

  • Category
  • Department
  • Item group

Sales are broken down to the level your reports need, whether that is a handful of categories or full item detail.

Tax and tips

  • Tax rates
  • Tips
  • Liability

Tax and tips are treated as money you owe, not income, so profit figures stay honest.

Card settlements

  • Net deposit
  • Fees
  • Bank match

Settlements post net of fees with the fees expensed separately, so the deposit clears against the bank feed without manual matching.

Voids and comps

  • Voids
  • Comps
  • Promotions

Discounts and giveaways are recorded on their own lines, which keeps gross revenue accurate and shows what promotions really cost.

Cash variances

  • Over and short
  • Cash drops
  • Till counts

Differences between expected and counted cash go to a dedicated account instead of vanishing into sales.

Late-night trading

  • Day close
  • Cutoff
  • Time zones

Venues that trade past midnight get a close time that matches how you actually count a day.

Multi-location businesses post one summary per site, tagged by QuickBooks class or location, so you can report per site and across the whole group.

Choosing a route

Four ways to build the link, and when each fits

Which route is right depends on your POS provider, whether you run QuickBooks Online or Desktop, and how many locations you have.

POS provider APISquare, Shopify POS, Lightspeed and similar

Best when you need daily totals, item sales, tips and settlement data.

Trade-off report timing varies by provider, so day-close cutoffs matter.

QuickBooks Online APICloud API secured with OAuth

Best when you post daily sales receipts or journal entries in QuickBooks Online.

Trade-off rate limits, and a production app review before go-live.

Web ConnectorQuickBooks Desktop, via QBXML

Best when a back-office team runs QuickBooks Desktop.

Trade-off it polls on a schedule and runs on the host machine.

iPaaS connectorsZapier, Make and vendor apps

Best when a single location has simple accounts.

Trade-off multiple locations and tip handling usually need custom logic.

If a ready-made app covers your setup, we will say so and help you configure it. Custom work earns its cost with several locations, tips, comps or QuickBooks Desktop.

How we work

From first call to monitored go-live

Every project follows the same five stages, and you approve the scope and the account mapping before anything is built.

  1. Discover

    We review your POS, locations, trading hours and chart of accounts.

  2. Map

    The daily journal structure, account splits and error rules are written down for sign-off.

  3. Build

    We develop against a QuickBooks test file or sandbox and a POS test account.

  4. Test

    Real trading days, including voids, comps, cash variances and late-night closes.

  5. Support

    Logging, alerts and a named contact after go-live.
