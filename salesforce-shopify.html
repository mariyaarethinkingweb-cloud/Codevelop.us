The problem

Why your store and your CRM only know half the customer

Shopify knows what a person bought. Salesforce knows the calls, cases and campaigns around them. With no link, agents keep Shopify open in a second tab to answer simple questions, and marketing builds segments from data that is weeks old.

The integration matches or creates a Salesforce contact for every Shopify buyer, writes order history against that record, keeps fulfilment and return status up to date, and adds lifetime value so sales, service and marketing all work from the same picture. The first choice we settle is whether orders land as standard Salesforce Orders or as a custom object.

Systems apart

  • Agents switching tabs to check orders
  • Buyers duplicated under different emails
  • Campaigns built on stale purchase data
  • Refunds missing from case history

Systems linked

  • Order history on the contact record
  • One matched record per customer
  • Segments built on current spend
  • Refunds and returns recorded automatically
Entity mapping

What syncs between Shopify and Salesforce, and which way

Most projects share the same core objects. We tune the matching rules and object design to the way your team works.

On ShopifyIn SalesforceDirectionWhat travels
CustomersContacts and accounts (or person accounts)Shopify → SalesforceName, email, phone and addresses, matched to avoid duplicates
OrdersOrders and order products, or a custom order objectShopify → SalesforceLine items, totals, discounts, taxes and shipping
FulfilmentsOrder status and tracking fieldsShopify → SalesforceShip dates, carriers and tracking numbers
Refunds and returnsOrder adjustments and related casesShopify → SalesforceRefunded amounts and reasons, so spend and case history stay correct
Products and variantsProducts (Product2) and price book entriesBoth waysCatalogue aligned on SKU so reports group by real products
Spend and order countLifetime value and order-count fieldsShopify → SalesforceTotals for segmentation, lead scoring and service priority

These are the usual directions. We change them to fit how you sell, for example by writing orders to a custom object when you already use Salesforce CPQ or Order Management.

After go-live

One Shopify order, from checkout to a resolved support case

Once the link is running, a typical order shows up in the CRM like this, with nobody exporting a report.

  1. Order placed

    A shopper checks out, and a webhook sends the order to the integration within moments.

  2. Customer matched

    The buyer is matched to an existing contact by email and phone, or created cleanly if they are new.

  3. Order recorded

    The order and its lines are written against the account, and lifetime value is updated.

  4. Shipment tracked

    When the order is fulfilled, the ship date and tracking number appear on the Salesforce order.

  5. Question answered

    If the customer calls or emails, the agent sees the order, tracking and any refund on the same screen and resolves the case.

Choosing a route

How we build the Shopify link

Shopify pushes events through webhooks, and Salesforce accepts them through REST or Bulk depending on volume. Busy stores need the batching decision made early.

RouteWhat it isSuitsKeep in mind
Shopify Admin API and webhooksGraphQL and REST access plus real-time order events.Syncing customers and orders the moment they happenWebhook delivery needs retry handling on your side
Salesforce REST APIA JSON API for real-time, record-level operations.Per-order sync at low or moderate volumeDaily call limits depend on edition, and high volume burns through them
Salesforce Bulk API 2.0Asynchronous loading of large data sets.Historical backfills and busy daily syncsNot real-time, because jobs queue before they run
iPaaS connectorsPrebuilt templates on tools such as Celigo, Workato or Make.Standard customer and order flows that must launch fastThe subscription never ends, and templates only bend so far

The decision that shapes everything is per-order sync versus daily batch. A store taking thousands of orders a day can exhaust Salesforce API limits with one call per record, so we size the design against your edition's allocation before choosing an approach.
