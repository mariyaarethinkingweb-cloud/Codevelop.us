The problem

Why EDI becomes a full-time job without automation

Large retailers and distributors do not accept orders by email. They send a structured 850 purchase order, and they expect an 856 ship notice and an 810 invoice back, in the right format and on time. Miss the window and the penalty is usually a chargeback, not just a late delivery.

Our integration translates each incoming purchase order into a B1 sales order, then turns your confirmed deliveries and A/R invoices into outbound documents. A 997 acknowledgment confirms receipt at every step, so you always know whether a document arrived or got stuck.

Entity mapping

Which EDI documents flow in and out of SAP B1

Every trading partner publishes its own spec, but most projects revolve around the same set of documents. We follow each partner's guide to the letter.

EDI documentIn SAP B1DirectionWhat travels
Purchase order (850)Sales orders (ORDR)Partner → B1Lines, quantities, ship-to locations, partner item numbers and prices
Order acknowledgment (855)Sales order status (ORDR)B1 → PartnerAccepted quantities, promised dates, line-level changes
Advance ship notice (856)Deliveries (ODLN)B1 → PartnerCarton and pallet detail, tracking numbers, GS1-128 label data
Invoice (810)A/R invoices (OINV)B1 → PartnerTotals and lines tied to the PO and ASN references
Functional acknowledgment (997)EDI message logBoth waysReceipt confirmation for each document, with failure alerts
Remittance and deductions (820, 812)Incoming payments (ORCT) and credit memos (ORIN)Partner → B1Payment detail and chargeback data logged against the original invoice

Document numbers shown are X12. If your partners use EDIFACT, the equivalents are ORDERS, ORDRSP, DESADV and INVOIC, and we map those the same way.

After go-live

One retailer order, from purchase order to paid invoice

Once the link is live, a typical order moves through your business like this, with no portal logins and no re-typing.

  1. Order received

    The retailer's 850 arrives, is checked against their spec, and is acknowledged with a 997 within minutes.

  2. Order created

    The translated order becomes a B1 sales order, with partner item numbers and prices matched to your own items.

  3. Shipment confirmed

    When the delivery is posted in B1, an 856 goes out with carton, pallet and label data before the retailer's deadline.

  4. Invoice sent

    The A/R invoice is converted into an 810 that matches the PO and ASN, so it clears the retailer's checks first time.

  5. Payment and deductions logged

    Remittance and chargeback data come back and are recorded against the original invoice for finance to review.

Choosing a route

How we build the EDI link

EDI has two separate problems: getting documents to and from your partner, and translating them. We solve both, using whichever transport your partner requires.

RouteWhat it isSuitsKeep in mind
VAN (value-added network)A managed EDI mailbox that partners already connect to.Retailers that require a specific networkPer-document fees add up at high volume
AS2 or SFTP directEncrypted point-to-point exchange with no middleman.Partners that accept a direct connectionCertificates must be managed on both sides
EDI translatorSoftware that converts X12 or EDIFACT to structured data and back.Every EDI project, whatever the transportPartner mapping guides must be followed exactly
SAP B1 Service Layer / DI APIInterfaces that create B1 orders, deliveries and invoices.Posting orders and producing outbound documentsPartner item cross-references need ongoing upkeep

A common shortcut that backfires: SAP's B1if framework can move EDI files, but with several non-SAP partners a dedicated translator working with the Service Layer is usually quicker to build and much easier to change when a partner updates its spec.

Our process

Five stages, with a signed mapping sheet before we build

You receive a fixed-price scope and approve the document mapping sheet before any configuration starts.

  1. Discover

    We read your partners' EDI specs and note the documents, transport and deadlines each one demands.

  2. Map

    Every segment, B1 field, item cross-reference and error rule is written down before building.

  3. Build

    We build the translator and B1 posting logic against a sandbox trading-partner setup.

  4. Test

    Partner-required test transactions run through to certification, 997 handling included.

  5. Support

    After launch we monitor with alerts on failed translations, and you get a named contact.
