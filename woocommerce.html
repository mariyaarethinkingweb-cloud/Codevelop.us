The basics

What WooCommerce integration actually means

It means your store and the rest of your software pass data to each other on their own: orders, customers, products and stock levels. One WooCommerce checkout can become an ERP sales order, an accounting invoice and a shipping label within seconds.

Most WooCommerce merchants also run an accounting tool, a shipping platform, a CRM and an email service, and sometimes an ERP. Each one is another place where orders get copied by hand. Connect them and you work from one picture, with fewer reconciliation errors and far fewer spreadsheet exports.

Without integration

  • Orders exported and retyped
  • Stock that's wrong by the afternoon
  • Labels and tracking added by hand
  • Gateway fees that never reconcile

With integration

  • Checkout creates the order everywhere
  • Stock updated on every channel
  • Tracking flows back to the customer
  • Invoices and deposits that match
Checkout to doorstep

  5. Customer updated

Self-hosted

Why WooCommerce integrates differently

Unlike hosted platforms, WooCommerce runs on your own server. That shapes every integration we design.

The advantageYou own the stack

What you gain is server-side logic inside WordPress, so data can be shaped before it leaves the site, with no platform gatekeeper in the way.

What it means a small piece of store code is often a cleaner fix than a workaround in middleware.

The responsibilityHosting decides reliability

What to watch is that webhook delivery, cron scheduling and speed all depend on how your hosting is set up.

What we do is audit caching, security plugins and cron first, then add retries to every build.

Order storageHPOS compatibility

What changed is that High-Performance Order Storage moves orders into dedicated tables for faster stores.

What we check is that any plugin or sync reading orders directly from the database is HPOS-compatible before we build.

Choosing a route

Five ways to connect WooCommerce, and when each one fits

WooCommerce gives you more ways in than a hosted platform: the REST API, webhooks, WordPress hooks, the Action Scheduler job queue, and iPaaS tools on top. Your order volume, active extensions and how much the data needs shaping decide which fits.

REST APIAuthenticated record access

Best when an outside system needs standard reads and writes of orders, products or customers.

Trade-off keys must be scoped correctly, and large queries need pagination.

WebhooksPush on store events

Best when orders should reach other systems the moment they're placed.

Trade-off delivery depends on your hosting, caching and security plugins.

WordPress hooksA custom plugin inside the store

Best when a complex store needs data shaped before it leaves the site.

Trade-off the code lives in your site and must survive core and plugin updates.

Action SchedulerWooCommerce's job queue

Best when you need retries, batching or long syncs without slowing checkout.

Trade-off it needs a real cron trigger, as WP-Cron alone is unreliable on quiet sites.

iPaaS connectorsZapier, Make, n8n or Celigo

Best when your order and customer flows are standard and you want them live fast.

Trade-off the subscription never ends, and templates only bend so far.

Because WooCommerce runs on your server, the cleanest fix is often a small piece of code inside WordPress. We build WordPress and WooCommerce sites too, so we can make that change on the store side.
