The problem

Why selling on both channels creates two sets of numbers

The moment a product is listed on Shopify and Amazon, it has two stock counts that drift apart. A sale on one channel does not reduce the other, so you oversell, cancel orders and damage your Amazon seller metrics.

The second problem is scattered data. Amazon orders, returns and settlement reports sit inside Seller Central while the rest of the business runs through Shopify and your accounting system. The integration pushes listings and stock from Shopify, pulls Amazon orders into one view, and splits each payout into sales, fees and refunds so finance can reconcile it. Depending on catalog size and fulfilment model, that runs on Amazon's Selling Partner API directly or on Shopify's own channel tools.

What syncs between Shopify and Amazon, and which way

Most projects share the same six data types. We tune each one to your catalog, your pricing rules and the way you fulfil orders.

These are the usual directions. We change them to fit your setup, for example by keeping FBA stock out of the sync entirely, because Amazon controls that count.

After go-live

One sale, on either channel, from order to reconciled payout

With the link running, a typical sale looks like this, and nobody opens Seller Central to update a number.

  1. Item sells

    A customer buys on Shopify or Amazon, and the order lands in your single order view.

  2. Stock updated

    The shared quantity drops, and the new figure, minus any safety buffer, is pushed to the other channel.

  3. Order fulfilled

    FBA orders ship from Amazon's warehouses, and FBM orders ship from yours with tracking sent back to the buyer.

  4. Return handled

    If the item comes back, the return reason is recorded and the stock is restored where it belongs.

  5. Payout reconciled

    Amazon's settlement report is split into sales, fees and refunds, and matched to what reached your bank.

Choosing a route

How we connect Shopify to Amazon

The right route depends on catalog size and fulfilment model. Larger catalogs usually need the direct API, and smaller ones are often well served by Shopify's own tooling.

RouteWhat it isSuitsKeep in mind
Shopify's Amazon sales channelA native app that syncs listings and orders with Amazon.Smaller catalogs with simple merchant fulfilmentLimited room for complex pricing or catalog rules
Amazon SP-APIAmazon's own API for listings, orders, inventory and reports.Large catalogs, repricing logic and FBA-heavy sellersNeeds developer approval and ongoing maintenance
Multi-channel iPaaSPrebuilt connectors linking Shopify with several marketplaces.Sellers on Amazon plus eBay, Walmart or othersA recurring subscription, and templates only bend so far
Custom middlewareOrchestration across Shopify, SP-API and your ERP or accounting tool.High SKU counts, mixed FBA/FBM and settlement matchingHighest build cost, worth it at real order volume

An honest note: for a small catalog fulfilled by you, Shopify's own Amazon channel is often enough. We will set that up properly before suggesting a custom SP-API build you do not need yet.
