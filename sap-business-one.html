The basics

What SAP Business One integration actually means

Integration lets SAP B1 and your other software pass records to each other automatically: customers, items, orders and invoices. A Shopify order becomes a B1 sales order, and a closed service job becomes an invoice, in minutes instead of days.

Most B1 companies also run five or more other tools, and each one is an island. Linking them gives you one picture of the business, less typing, and none of the slips that come from copying numbers by hand.

Without integration

  • Orders retyped into B1 by hand
  • Stock counts drift between systems
  • Invoices wait days to go out
  • Reports rebuilt in spreadsheets

With integration

  • Orders post themselves
  • One live stock number everywhere
  • Invoices raised when work completes
  • One view for sales and finance

Choosing a route

Five ways to connect SAP B1, and when each one wins

B1 ships with its own interfaces, and third-party platforms wrap them in ready-made connectors. The right pick depends on your B1 version, database, transaction volume and how standard your process is.

Service LayerREST / OData API

Best when volumes are high or you need near real-time sync.

Trade-off needs real development effort.

DI APICOM-based object access

Best when you run SQL Server or an older B1 version.

Trade-off slower at scale and older technology.

Integration FrameworkB1if, SAP's bundled tool

Best when you connect SAP to SAP or run intercompany flows.

Trade-off steep learning curve and awkward for non-SAP apps.

Ready-made connectoriPaaS template

Best when your flow is standard and you want to go live fast.

Trade-off monthly subscription and limited flexibility.

Custom middlewareMake, n8n or code

Best when several systems or unusual rules are involved.

Trade-off you need a partner to maintain it.

A ready-made connector is often the right answer for a standard flow. When it is, we'll tell you, then help you choose and configure it.
