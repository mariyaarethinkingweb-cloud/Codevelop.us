SAP Business One and Zuper, running off one set of records.

We connect your ERP to Zuper so customers, equipment, parts and contracts reach your technicians on their own, and every finished job lands back in B1 ready to bill. The sync is shaped around how your crews actually work, not squeezed into a fixed template.

The problem

Two systems, two versions of the truth

SAP B1 knows the money side of your service business: customers, price lists, stock and invoices. Zuper knows the work side: jobs, technicians, schedules and what was found on site. When they don't talk, the office retypes every job and crews drive out on stale customer and parts data.

Connect them and dispatchers schedule against live ERP records, technicians open a job and see the equipment history, and a closed job turns into a B1 document without a second round of typing. For a team closing hundreds of jobs a month, that is a lot of admin time back and invoices that go out sooner.

Systems apart

  • Jobs retyped from B1 into Zuper
  • Quotes based on outdated stock
  • Parts used never reach the ERP
  • Invoices wait on paperwork

Systems linked

  • Service calls show up as jobs in minutes
  • Van stock matches B1 warehouses
  • Parts post to the right warehouse
  • Billing drafted when the job closes
Entity mapping

What moves between SAP B1 and Zuper

Every record a field operation leans on can travel: customers, service calls, equipment, parts, warehouses, contracts and billing. Direction and trigger are set per record, to match how your teams really work.

These are the usual directions. If crews create new customers on site we add a reverse flow for business partners, and if billing lives in Zuper the invoice flow turns around. The mapping bends to your process, not the other way round.

Field-level detail

What travels inside each record

Before anything is built, you sign off a field-by-field sheet. These are the standard fields we map for each area.

Customers and sites

  • Card code
  • Contacts
  • Ship-to sites

Name, contacts, payment terms and customer group. Each ship-to address becomes a Zuper property, so crews arrive at the right site.

Service calls and jobs

  • Status
  • Technician
  • Notes

Subject, priority, customer, equipment, schedule and resolution notes. A status change in Zuper writes back to the B1 call.

Equipment

  • Serials
  • Warranty
  • History

Serial numbers, linked item code, install date and warranty or contract link, so technicians see the past before they knock.

Parts and items

  • Pricing
  • Units
  • Stock

Code, description, unit of measure, price list values and per-warehouse quantities. Parts used on a job reduce the correct B1 warehouse.

Van and truck stock

  • Warehouses
  • Vehicles
  • Counts

B1 warehouse codes map to Zuper stock locations, with each vehicle modelled as its own warehouse, so van counts finally match SAP.

Orders and billing

  • Sales orders
  • Invoices
  • Due dates

A finished job creates a B1 sales order or invoice draft with lines, quantities, unit prices and warehouse codes already filled in.

Beyond the standard set, we also map user-defined fields, job photos, signatures and custom checklists. That is the layer where off-the-shelf templates tend to run out.

After go-live

One job, from first call to invoice

Once the link is live, an everyday service job runs like this, with nobody re-keying anything and no paperwork waiting in a tray.

  1. Call logged

    A breakdown comes in and the office records a service call in B1 against the customer and their equipment card.

  2. Job created

    Within a minute a matching Zuper job exists, with the site, asset history and priority attached.

  3. Crew dispatched

    Dispatch assigns a technician, who reviews past visits and checks parts against live van stock.

  4. Job closed

    The technician logs parts, takes photos and a signature, and closes the job. Status and notes return to the B1 call.

  5. Billing drafted

    Parts post to the right warehouse and a sales order or invoice draft appears in B1, ready the same day.

Choosing a route

Three ways to build the Zuper link

Zuper offers a REST API with webhooks. On the B1 side we use the Service Layer (HANA, and SQL from version 10) or the DI API for older SQL installs. In our experience Zuper has no built-in B1 connector, so the link runs through middleware of some kind.

Vendor templatePrebuilt B1 to Zuper iPaaS flow

Best when your process matches the standard records and you want the quickest launch.

Trade-off a monthly subscription and little room for custom fields or rules.

Orchestrated buildMake or n8n across both APIs

Best when you need a standard core plus approvals, custom rules or a third system in the chain.

Trade-off a modest platform cost, and someone has to own it.

Custom middlewareService Layer plus Zuper API and webhooks

Best when volumes are high, transformations are complex or the server is on premise.

Trade-off a bigger build, so you will want a support retainer.

If a vendor template covers everything you need, buying it is the right call and we will configure it for you. Most service businesses we scope are about 80% standard and 20% custom, and that 20% (user-defined fields, approvals, van stock rules, photo handling) is where an independent partner earns its fee.
