The problem

Why finance finds out about problems last

Finance owns the receivables, but the customer relationship lives in Salesforce. Invoices get built from a deal summary pasted into an email, and by the time an invoice is 45 days late, the account manager has already booked the next meeting without knowing.

The integration builds QuickBooks invoices directly from approved Salesforce deals, with the correct customer, terms, tax and line items. It then returns invoice status, open balance, aging buckets and overdue flags to the account, and can raise a task for the owner when a payment slips. Finance stops chasing people for information, and the sales team stops being surprised.

Systems apart

  • Invoices built from emailed deal notes
  • Wrong terms or tax on first invoices
  • Late payers noticed after 60 days
  • Account owners unaware of open balances

Systems linked

  • Invoices generated from approved deals
  • Terms and tax pulled from the customer record
  • Aging visible on the Salesforce account
  • Tasks raised for owners when payments slip

We start from the finance records and work outward, agreeing which system is the source of truth for each before anything is built.

These are the usual flows. Because billing data is the authority here, QuickBooks normally owns terms and balances, while Salesforce owns the relationship and the deal.

After go-live

One invoice, from approved deal to collected cash

Once the link is running, a typical invoice moves through both systems like this, and finance only steps in when something needs a decision.

  1. Deal approved

    An opportunity reaches its approved stage, and the customer is matched against QuickBooks so terms and tax come from the right record.

  2. Invoice drafted

    QuickBooks receives an invoice with the correct lines and payment terms, and its number is written back to the deal.

  3. Invoice sent

    Finance reviews and sends it. The due date and PDF link now sit on the Salesforce account for the owner to see.

  4. Payment slips

    If the due date passes, the aging bucket changes and a task is raised for the account owner, well before the 60-day mark.

  5. Cash collected

    When payment is recorded, the balance clears on the account and any overdue task is closed automatically.

How we build the QuickBooks side of the link

Salesforce is flexible in both directions, so the QuickBooks edition and your company-file setup drive most of the design.

Web Connector (Desktop, QBXML)An Intuit utility that exchanges QBXML with a local company file.Desktop customers who need a link to a cloud CRMPolls on a schedule, from the machine hosting the file
Salesforce REST, Bulk and Platform EventsRecord-level, batch and event-driven access to Salesforce data.Triggering invoices on approval and writing aging backDaily call limits depend on your Salesforce edition
iPaaS connectorsPrebuilt templates on tools such as Celigo, Workato or Zapier.A simple invoice push that has to be live quicklyOngoing subscription, with limited room for aging and task logic

Most of the value for finance sits in the return path: aging, balances and overdue triggers. Many connectors push an invoice but never send that data back, so we check what a template really covers before recommending one.
