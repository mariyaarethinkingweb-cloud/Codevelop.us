The problem

Why reps quote one thing and the ERP says another

Deals are worked in Salesforce, but stock, pricing and financials live in B1. With no link, reps quote from last week's price list, and someone in the back office retypes every closed-won opportunity as a sales order, often without checking whether the stock is there.

The integration keeps accounts and business partners aligned, posts won opportunities into B1 as sales orders, and sends stock levels, prices, delivery progress and invoice data back to Salesforce. A rep can answer a customer's question without opening the ERP or interrupting someone in operations.

Systems apart

  • Quotes built from stale price lists
  • Won deals retyped into B1
  • Stock promised without checking
  • Reps asking operations for delivery news

Systems linked

  • Live prices and stock inside Salesforce
  • Sales orders created from won deals
  • Credit limits visible before terms are agreed
  • Delivery and payment status on the account
Entity mapping

What syncs between Salesforce and SAP B1, and which way

Most projects share the same core objects. The usual starting rule is that B1 stays the system of record for anything financial.

In SalesforceIn SAP B1DirectionWhat travels
Accounts and contactsBusiness partners (OCRD)Both waysNames, addresses, payment terms, matched on a shared reference
Products and price booksItems (OITM) and price lists (OPLN)B1 → SalesforceItem master, price lists and available quantities (OITW)
Opportunities and quotesSales orders (ORDR)Salesforce → B1Closed-won deals and approved quotes, with lines and discounts
Order status fieldsDeliveries (ODLN)B1 → SalesforceDelivery progress, ship dates and tracking numbers
Account finance fieldsA/R invoices (OINV) and incoming payments (ORCT)B1 → SalesforceInvoice totals, open balance and payment status
Credit warningsCredit limit and block on the business partnerB1 → SalesforceCredit blocks shown before a rep promises terms the ERP will refuse

These are the usual directions. Prospects start in Salesforce, so we set a clear point where an account is promoted to a B1 business partner, and from then on B1 owns the financial fields.

After go-live

One quote, from live stock check to paid invoice

With the link running, a typical deal moves through both systems like this, and nobody retypes a line.

  1. Quote built

    The rep builds the quote from current B1 prices and sees available stock for each line before sending it.

  2. Deal won

    When the opportunity closes, the account is checked against B1 business partners and promoted if it is new.

  3. Order posted

    A sales order is created in B1 with the right lines, prices and terms, and its number is written back to Salesforce.

  4. Goods delivered

    Once the delivery is posted in B1, the ship date and tracking appear on the Salesforce account.

  5. Invoice and payment

    The invoice, balance and payment status update on the account, so sales and customer success can answer billing questions themselves.

Choosing a route

How we build the Salesforce link

B1 offers the Service Layer and the DI API, and Salesforce offers REST, Bulk and Platform Events. A middleware tier between them is almost always the right design.

RouteWhat it isSuitsKeep in mind
SAP B1 Service LayerA REST/OData API for B1 business objects.Modern builds, higher volumes and real-time postingHANA-first, and needs real development skill
SAP B1 DI APIA COM-based API for reading and writing B1 objects.SQL deployments and older environmentsOlder technology that slows down at scale
Salesforce REST and Platform EventsRecord-level access plus event-driven publishing.Triggering on close and pushing status back to the accountDaily call limits depend on your edition
Custom middlewareOrchestration built with n8n, Make or code between the two systems.Queueing, retries, transformation and error visibilityNeeds a partner who can maintain it

Why not a direct link? ERP writes fail for real business reasons, such as a credit block, a closed posting period or an item that no longer exists. A middleware tier lets those failures queue, retry and raise an alert instead of disappearing between two systems.
