The basics

What Salesforce integration actually means

It means Salesforce and your other software trade records on their own: accounts, contacts, opportunities, orders and invoices. A closed-won deal becomes a NetSuite sales order, a QuickBooks payment shows up on the account, and a Shopify buyer lands in your CRM at checkout.

Most Salesforce teams also run an ERP, an accounting tool, a store and a marketing platform, and each one holds a slice of the customer. Connect them and sales sees fulfilment and payment status, finance sees the pipeline, and the weekly hours of re-keying and copy-paste errors disappear.

Without integration

  • Won deals retyped as orders
  • Reps can't see payment status
  • One customer keyed into four tools
  • Pipeline numbers finance doesn't trust

With integration

  • Closed-won creates the sales order
  • Shipping and payment status on the account
  • One customer record everywhere
  • One set of numbers for sales and finance
Closed-won to cash

One deal, five handoffs, zero re-keying

  1. Deal closes

    The rep marks the opportunity won in Salesforce.

  2. Order created

    The ERP receives the account, products and terms.

  3. Invoice issued

    Accounting raises the invoice from the same order.

  4. Payment lands

    The payment is matched to the right invoice.

  5. Account updated

    Status flows back, so the rep sees it without asking.

Choosing a route

Five ways to connect Salesforce, and when each one fits

Salesforce has one of the widest API sets around, and iPaaS platforms like MuleSoft wrap them in ready-made connectors. Your edition, data volume, API limits and process all shape the choice.

REST APIJSON, record by record

Best when you need real-time sync or single-record updates.

Trade-off daily call limits depend on your edition.

Bulk API 2.0Asynchronous batches

Best when you migrate data or sync millions of records overnight.

Trade-off jobs are queued, so it isn't real-time.

Platform EventsStreaming, event-driven

Best when downstream systems must react the moment a record changes.

Trade-off event allocations apply, and something must subscribe.

MuleSoft or iPaaSCeligo, Boomi, Workato

Best when your flow is standard and you want it live fast.

Trade-off licence cost never ends, and MuleSoft is priced for enterprises.

Custom middlewareMake, n8n or code

Best when several systems or unusual rules are involved.

Trade-off you need a partner to look after it.

For many standard flows a ready-made connector wins, even MuleSoft or Celigo. We'll say so, and only build custom when your process needs it.
