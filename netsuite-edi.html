NetSuite and EDI, so retailer orders arrive, ship and invoice on their own.

We connect NetSuite to your trading partners so purchase orders, ship notices and invoices travel as structured EDI documents instead of emails and spreadsheets. Orders become NetSuite records, and fulfilments and invoices go back out in the exact format each partner demands.

The problem

Big buyers will not accept a PDF, and a late document costs real money

Large retailers and distributors trade through structured EDI documents, not phone calls or attachments. They also enforce deadlines, so a missed acknowledgment or a late ship notice usually ends in a chargeback, not just a delayed shipment.

Connect NetSuite to the EDI flow and each step is handled for you. An incoming purchase order becomes a sales order, fulfilments become ship notices, invoices go out matched to the order, and every document is acknowledged so you know it arrived.

Manual EDI

  • Orders keyed in from a portal
  • Ship notices built by hand
  • Acknowledgments missed or late
  • Chargebacks found weeks afterwards

Integrated EDI

  • Orders create sales orders themselves
  • Ship notices sent when you fulfil
  • Every document acknowledged
  • Deductions logged against the invoice
Documents

The EDI documents that move between your partners and NetSuite

Each document has a direction and a purpose in the order cycle. Your partners' own specifications decide the exact segments, and we follow them to the letter.

DocumentWhat it doesDirectionIn NetSuite
850 Purchase orderPartner places an order, mapped with partner-specific items and pricesPartner → NetSuiteSales order
855 Order acknowledgmentConfirms accepted quantities, dates and any line changesNetSuite → PartnerOrder confirmation
856 Advance ship noticeCarton and pallet detail, tracking numbers and label dataNetSuite → PartnerItem fulfilment
810 InvoiceGenerated from the NetSuite invoice and matched to the order and ship noticeNetSuite → PartnerInvoice
997 Functional acknowledgmentConfirms receipt, and a failure raises an immediate alertBoth waysTransmission log
Chargebacks and remittanceDeduction data such as 812 and 820 style documents is logged against the original invoicePartner → NetSuitePayment and adjustment records

We support X12 and the EDIFACT equivalents (ORDERS, ORDRSP, DESADV and INVOIC), so the same approach works for North American and European partners.

In practice

What your team stops doing by hand

Each partner has its own rulebook. This is how the common requirements are handled inside the flow.

Order intake

  • 850
  • Item cross-reference
  • Pricing

Partner item numbers are translated to your own, and prices are checked, so the sales order lands ready to fulfil.

Acknowledgments

  • 855
  • 997
  • Line changes

Accepted quantities and dates go back promptly, and a failed receipt raises an alert instead of passing quietly.

Ship notices and labels

  • 856
  • Cartons
  • GS1-128

Carton and pallet structure, tracking numbers and the data for compliant shipping labels are produced from the fulfilment.

Invoicing

  • 810
  • PO match
  • ASN match

Invoices are generated from NetSuite and matched to the order and ship notice, which is what retailers check before paying.

Compliance timing

  • Deadlines
  • Routing
  • Penalties

Ship notice timing and retailer routing rules are built into the order and fulfilment flow, so you avoid the avoidable fines.

Deductions

  • Chargebacks
  • Remittance
  • Reasons

Deduction and remittance data is logged against the original invoice, so finance can dispute or accept with the evidence at hand.

Partner item cross-references need ongoing care. We document who maintains them so a new product does not break the flow.

Choosing a route

The building blocks, and when each fits

An EDI project combines a way of reaching your partners, a translator that speaks their format and the logic that posts into NetSuite. Every project needs the translator, and the other pieces depend on your partners.

Value-added networkHosted exchange between partners

Best when a partner requires a specific network.

Trade-off per-document fees add up at high volume.

AS2 or SFTP directPoint-to-point connection

Best when partners accept direct connections without network fees.

Trade-off certificates have to be managed on both sides.

EDI translatorConverts documents to and from your data

Best when always, whatever the transport.

Trade-off the partner's mapping guide must be followed exactly.

SuiteTalk and RESTletsPosting into NetSuite

Best when creating sales orders, fulfilments and invoices.

Trade-off partner item cross-references must be kept current.

A NetSuite EDI app suits standard retail programmes. Custom translation pays off when partner specs, labelling or item cross-references do not fit a template.

How we work

From partner spec to monitored go-live

Every project follows the same five stages, and you approve the scope and the mapping before anything is built.

  1. Discover

    We review each partner's EDI specification, document types and transport needs.

  2. Map

    Segments, NetSuite fields, item cross-references and error rules are written down for sign-off.

  3. Build

    We build the translator and the NetSuite posting logic in a sandbox.

  4. Test

    We run the partner's required test transactions through certification, including acknowledgment handling.

  5. Support

    Alerts on failed translations, plus a named contact after go-live.
